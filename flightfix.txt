# Cyphorto Drone AI V2 — Fix Overflight and Ground Bouncing

You are working on my autonomous Uncrashed drone pilot in `drone-ai-v2`.

The flight controller, launcher, and real-time monitoring system are now operational. Your task is to improve the drone's actual flight performance.

**Your two priorities are:**

1. Eliminate or substantially reduce overflight failures where the drone passes above a racing gate, loses sight of it, and eventually times out.
2. Fix ground-bouncing behavior where the drone repeatedly hits or bounces along the ground instead of maintaining controlled flight or recovering.

The ultimate goal is reliable completion of the Roadtrip racecourse, followed by generalization to other maps.

## Current project state

- The working controller is `p2_fast_baseline`.
- The V2 launcher is `start_roadtrip.ps1`.
- The live monitor already records flight telemetry and reset events.
- Historical flights include a successful 63-gate completion.
- Recent baseline attempts reached 25, 2, 32, and 23 gates without finishing.
- The experimental `p2_side_glimpse` candidate reached 57 gates but did not demonstrate that its proposed fix actually activated.
- The dominant observed failure is overflight. The target gate moves below the camera frame, and the pilot continues flying forward without reacquiring it.
- The previous lateral-glimpse fix addressed a less common failure mode and remains unproven.

Preserve existing successful behavior and training history.

## Phase 1 — Diagnose both problems

Before editing algorithms, inspect the relevant source files, flight telemetry, screenshots, and recorded attempts.

### Problem A — Overflight

Determine why the drone passes above gates instead of through them.

Investigate:

- Gate alignment and vertical position in the camera frame.
- Camera tilt and its relationship to actual flight direction.
- Pitch, throttle, speed, and altitude near gate crossings.
- Whether the drone begins descending too late.
- Whether the pilot incorrectly predicts a successful crossing.
- Whether gate-target selection changes prematurely.
- Why the drone continues forward after losing the target.
- Whether the existing crossing timeout prevents recovery.

Examine the last several seconds before each documented overflight.

Distinguish confirmed causes from hypotheses.

### Problem B — Ground bouncing

Investigate why the drone sometimes repeatedly collides with or bounces along the ground.

Determine whether the primary cause is:

- Excessive downward velocity.
- Incorrect throttle or pitch commands.
- Poor altitude estimation.
- Delayed collision detection.
- An unstable recovery controller.
- Control oscillations after ground contact.
- Attempts to continue forward while insufficiently airborne.

Inspect relevant damage events, flight states, controller outputs, and simulator telemetry.

Do not assume that a successful impact recovery means the underlying behavior is acceptable.

## Phase 2 — Improve altitude and gate alignment

Implement targeted changes supported by the evidence.

Potential improvements include:

1. Predictive vertical alignment before reaching a gate.
2. Speed-dependent pitch and throttle adjustment.
3. Better altitude control during gate approaches.
4. Detection when the target gate moves beneath the camera frame.
5. Controlled target reacquisition after an overflight.
6. Better handling of uncertain gate crossings.

Avoid relying on fixed Roadtrip coordinates.

The controller should use visual observations and available flight state whenever possible.

Do not blindly increase or decrease throttle. Respect the existing control conventions and verify the effects in simulation.

## Phase 3 — Improve ground-contact recovery

Develop or refine recovery behavior so the drone can stabilize after a ground collision.

When ground contact is detected:

1. Determine whether the drone is still controllable.
2. Prevent unnecessary repeated impact cycles.
3. Reduce destabilizing control commands.
4. Recover sufficient altitude when physically feasible.
5. Reestablish stable attitude and movement.
6. Resume gate navigation once conditions are suitable.

Include hysteresis or other stabilization measures if supported by the observed failure.

Avoid rapid switching between normal flight and recovery modes.

Do not endlessly attempt recovery when the drone is irrecoverably stuck. Preserve a bounded, diagnosable failure state.

## Phase 4 — Implement carefully

Create a new experimental controller version, such as:

`p2_overflight_recovery`

Keep `p2_fast_baseline` untouched.

Prefer minimal changes to existing flight logic rather than rewriting the pilot.

Add focused regression tests for:

- Gate disappearing below the camera frame.
- Incorrect crossing detection.
- Overflight timeout and reacquisition.
- Ground-contact detection.
- Repeated bouncing.
- Stable transition from recovery back to navigation.

Use historical telemetry and recorded images for offline testing wherever possible.

Do not build another launcher, monitoring framework, or unnecessary infrastructure.

## Phase 5 — Real simulator testing

Use the existing Uncrashed session on Roadtrip.

Run the experimental controller with the working launcher and monitor, adapting only the configuration necessary to select the candidate.

Perform a maximum of three supervised simulator attempts initially.

For each attempt, record:

- Gates passed.
- Successful race completion.
- Total flight duration.
- Overflight events.
- Ground impacts and bouncing episodes.
- Successful recoveries.
- Gate reacquisition attempts and outcomes.
- Final failure reason.

Review each attempt's telemetry immediately after completion.

If a flight fails, identify the cause before modifying parameters.

Do not repeat identical unsuccessful attempts indefinitely.

## Phase 6 — Compare with the baseline

Compare the candidate against the unchanged baseline using consistent configurations and comparable conditions.

Remember that the baseline has already shown significant run-to-run variance. A single high gate count does not prove improvement.

Evaluate whether:

- Overflight frequency decreases.
- Ground bouncing decreases.
- Gate reacquisition succeeds more frequently.
- Race completion becomes more consistent.
- Previously working recovery behavior is preserved.

Do not promote the candidate unless the evidence supports it.

If the results are inconclusive, preserve the experiment and report that further testing is necessary.

## Phase 7 — Prepare for other maps

Ensure any improvements are based on general flight behavior rather than special-case Roadtrip instructions.

Avoid hardcoded gate numbers, predetermined routes, or memorized coordinates.

Once Roadtrip completion improves, prepare a separate validation strategy for Canyon.

Do not automatically start Canyon training during this task.

## Operating constraints

- Preserve all existing training data.
- Do not overwrite historical checkpoints.
- Do not modify the original successful pilot.
- Do not launch a second Uncrashed instance.
- Use existing monitoring and launch infrastructure.
- Do not make paid Meta Model API calls.
- Do not modify unrelated code.
- Do not introduce unnecessary dependencies.
- Do not confuse a longer flight with a proven algorithmic improvement.
- Keep a clear record of every algorithm change.
- Run automated tests before simulator flights.

You may autonomously inspect files, fix code, run tests, and perform the limited supervised flight evaluations described above.

## Final deliverables

Provide:

1. Root causes of the overflight and bouncing problems, with supporting evidence.
2. Exact files and algorithms changed.
3. Regression test results.
4. Before-and-after flight comparisons.
5. Whether Roadtrip was completed.
6. Whether the candidate should replace the baseline.
7. Remaining limitations and the next recommended improvement.

**Your priority is reliable flight, not maximum speed.**

Begin by investigating the overflight failures and ground-contact telemetry from existing runs. Identify the smallest changes likely to address both problems, implement them in a separate experimental version, and validate them in Uncrashed.
